## What are the main Agile ceremonies, and what is their purpose?
Sprint Planning, the Daily Stand-up, the Sprint Review, and the Sprint Retrospective

**Daily Stand-up**
A short daily sync (usually 15 minutes) where each team member answers three questions:
what did I do yesterday, what will I do today, and are there any blockers.
The goal is to keep the team aligned, surface and resolve issues early,
and give the PM the visibility needed to effectively plan and adjust upcoming tasks.

**Sprint Planning vs. Continuous Prioritization**
At the start of each sprint, the team selects tasks from the backlog and agrees on
what will be delivered by the end of the sprint.
Continuous Prioritization is typically managed using Kanban — there are no fixed sprints;
instead, tasks are continuously re-prioritised and pulled into work as capacity becomes available.
This makes it more flexible and better suited to handling changing requirements,
such as a task getting blocked or a client raising an urgent last-minute request.

**Retrospective**
Held at the end of each sprint, the team reflects together on what went well, what needs improvement, and what specific changes to make in the next sprint.
The goal is to continuously improve the team's ways of working, increase efficiency, and encourage honest reflection.

**Backlog Refinement**
A regular session to review and clean up the backlog — breaking down large tasks,
adding detail to requirements, estimating effort, and adjusting priorities. The goal is to ensure tasks are clear and well-defined so the team can hit the ground running
in Sprint Planning without needing last-minute clarification.


## How Agile Teams Collaborate Asynchronously Across Time Zones

Agile teams replace status meetings with asynchronous communication such as recorded video updates,
manage tasks on shared digital boards, limit real-time chat and necessary team meetings to a short daily overlap window, and rely on thorough written documentation
to keep projects running around the clock.

Retrospectives are conducted on shared digital whiteboards (e.g. Jira), where everyone can add cards,
vote, and leave comments within a 24-hour window before action items are finalised — no need for simultaneous participation. This ensures that team members across all time zones
can stay informed about task updates and challenges that occurred while they were offline, simply by reading the documentation.

The most important factor in cross-timezone collaboration is identifying a 2–3 hour window
when all time zones overlap, reserved specifically for urgent discussions or resolving complex problems that require real-time input.

# Reflection

## How do Agile ceremonies help with communication and alignment?
Agile ceremonies — including daily stand-ups, sprint planning, retrospectives,and backlog refinement — help teams communicate effectively and stay aligned.

They keep the team in sync in the following ways:
daily stand-ups allow the team to quickly share progress and blockers each day,
preventing issues from being hidden for too long and ensuring everyone is aware of the current state of the project.
Sprint planning helps the team reach a shared understanding of goals and priorities at the start of each sprint, avoiding misalignment.

## Which ceremony do you think is most important for your role, and why?

Among these, I believe backlog refinement is the most important —
it ensures tasks are clearly defined and properly prioritised,
reducing ambiguity and rework during execution.
