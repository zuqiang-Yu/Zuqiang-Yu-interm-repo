# Professionalism at Focus Bear - Reflection

## Research & Learn

### Professional vs. unprofessional behaviour in a casual work environment

In a relaxed workplace, professional behaviour means replying to messages in a reasonable time, joining meetings on time and prepared, and sharing progress openly with the team. Unprofessional behaviour includes ignoring messages, arriving late or unprepared, talking over others, and gossiping or blaming teammates. Being casual and having fun is fine, as long as I stay respectful and accountable for my work.

### Respectful communication in remote teams

In remote teams, written messages can easily be misread, so I keep my tone polite and friendly and give enough context so others don't have to guess what I mean. When there is a disagreement, I focus on the issue rather than the person and move sensitive topics to a private chat or a quick call. When I receive feedback, I thank the person, ask questions if something is unclear, and act on it.

### Giving and receiving feedback professionally

Good feedback focuses on actions rather than people, for example saying "this function doesn't handle empty input" instead of "your code is bad". When receiving feedback, I assume good intent, because most blunt comments are not personal. If feedback is unclear, I ask for clarification instead of reacting defensively.

### Key points from the guide for working with supervisors

The guide explains that every supervisor has their own style, but they all value clear communication, initiative and good vibes. This means I should keep my supervisor updated, try to solve problems before asking for help, and stay positive and easy to work with.

## Reflection

### Have you ever experienced or witnessed great teamwork and professionalism? What made it effective?

Yes, I experienced great teamwork during my university final year project. Our project manager assigned tasks based on the project timeline and how different sub-teams depended on each other, so no one was left waiting. The project manager also communicated promptly with the client whenever requirements changed and passed feedback back to the team quickly. As a result, the project was delivered successfully and is now in real use.

### What steps can you take to make sure your communication is clear, respectful, and inclusive?

I address people by name and use a polite tone, especially when asking for help. As a non-native English speaker, I use translation and AI tools to make sure I understand others and that my messages are clear. For complex issues, I share screenshots or screen recordings because they are easier to follow than long text. I also respect colleagues from different cultural backgrounds.

### How can you help create a positive, fun, and professional work environment?

I thank teammates when they help me and recognise their good work. I also use emojis and reactions in chat to keep communication light and friendly. Replying to messages promptly is another way I show teammates that I support them.

### How can you prepare effectively for meetings with supervisors and colleagues?

Before a meeting, I read the agenda, prepare my questions, and organise my progress. When reporting, I explain clearly what I have done and what I plan to do next. Since English is not my first language, I also prepare my wording in advance for anything important.

### What would it look like to be proactive during your internship?

When I run into a problem, I research and try to solve it myself first, and only ask for help if I am still stuck. When I finish a task, I look for the next one instead of waiting to be assigned. I also update my progress regularly so my supervisor doesn't need to ask.

### How might you follow up with colleagues or supervisors if you need information or action from them? How would you escalate it further, and when?

In my first request, I give detailed information about the background, what happened, and exactly what help I need. If there is no reply after one or two days and the matter isn't urgent, I keep working on other tasks. After two days, I look for help from other teammates and send a polite follow-up to the original person. If the issue is urgent or still unresolved, I escalate it to my supervisor.

### What are some things you won't do?

I won't discriminate against anyone or criticise teammates in public. I won't pretend to understand something when I don't, and I will ask questions instead. I also won't hide my mistakes, but will own them and fix them.
