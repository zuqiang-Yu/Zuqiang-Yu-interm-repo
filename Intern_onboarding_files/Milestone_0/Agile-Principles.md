# Agile Principles & Frameworks

##  Research & Learn

### What is Agile, and how does it differ from traditional project management?

Agile is an iterative approach to project management that focuses on delivering small, working increments of a product frequently, rather than delivering everything at the end of a long project. 

Traditional (Waterfall) project management follows a fixed, sequential process — requirements are locked upfront, and the product is only delivered after all phases (design, build, test) are complete. 

### What are the core values and principles of Agile?

1. **Individuals and interactions** over processes and tools
2. **Working software** over comprehensive documentation
3. **Customer collaboration**, need to meeting or communicate with client frequently
4. **Responding to change** over following a plan


### What are the differences between Scrum and Kanban, and when is each used?

Scrum organises work into fixed-length sprints (1–2 weeks), with defined roles (Scrum Master, Product Owner, Dev Team) and regular ceremonies (stand-ups, sprint planning, retrospectives). It works best when the team has predictable, plannable work and needs a clear delivery rhythm.

Kanban uses a continuous flow model with no fixed iterations. Work is pulled from a backlog as capacity allows, and the focus is on limiting work-in-progress (WIP) to reduce bottlenecks. It suits teams with unpredictable or ongoing work, such as support, maintenance, or fast-changing priorities.

### Why does Focus Bear lean towards Kanban instead of Scrum?

Focus Bear leans towards Kanban because it suits a small, fast-moving team. As a startup, priorities can shift quickly — a critical bug or urgent feature may need to jump the queue at any time. Kanban's flexible, continuous flow allows the team to respond to these changes without being locked into a sprint commitment.

## Reflection

### What do you think are the biggest benefits and challenges of Agile?

The biggest benefit of Agile is adaptability — the ability to respond to user feedback and changing requirements without derailing the entire project. Frequent releases also mean users get value sooner, and problems are caught early. 

The main challenge is that Agile requires strong communication and discipline. Without clear priorities and good collaboration between roles, it's easy for scope to creep or for teams to lose direction between iterations.

### How can Agile principles help improve your role and responsibilities?

As a Developer, Agile encourages me to break work into small, deliverable chunks rather than building large features in isolation.
